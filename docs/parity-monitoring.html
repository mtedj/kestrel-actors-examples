What it found at 44 independent US hotels this week

We ran the check on 44 well-known independents in New York, DC, Austin, Miami, LA, SF, Portland, Boston, Denver, New Orleans and Chicago — 14 consecutive check-in dates each, 1 night, 2 adults, exactly as a traveller comparing prices on Google sees it.

The undercutting sites are rarely Booking.com or Expedia. They are wholesale resellers — Super.com, Vio.com, Etrip, Traveluro, Evendo, Billabook and a few dozen others — selling inventory that leaked through a bed bank, and on several nights the major OTAs were under the direct rate too. A traveller on Google does not know the difference: they see your site at $239 and a site they have never heard of at $135.

How it works

  • Every morning we read the price comparison Google Hotels shows for your property and each competitor, for the next 14 check-in dates (or the window you choose), in your currency and market.
  • Rates are the lowest each site shows for the stay — the number the traveller compares. Rate plans and room types differ between sites; the point is what the traveller is shown, not what you loaded.
  • Nothing is booked, no rooms are held, no login to your systems is needed. It is public data, read the way a browser reads it.
  • Your history accumulates: every run appends dated rows, so "how often were we undercut in September" is a filter, not a project.
