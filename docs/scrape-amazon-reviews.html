How to scrape Amazon reviews, and what the real limit is

This Actor reads only the review sample visible on an accessible public product page, not full review history. UK examples were verified on 2026-11-07; current US review requests hit a sign-in wall. Availability and review counts vary by product and marketplace.

The number nobody puts in the listing

Public Amazon product pages can expose a limited review sample, and some requests require sign-in. There is no universal minimum or maximum. On 2026-11-07, two UK pages exposed 5 and 8 reviews before filters and caps; US review requests remained sign-in blocked. These samples do not represent full review history.

A /product-reviews/<ASIN>/ request can return a sign-in page even with HTTP 200. The Actor checks the response body and reports a blocked request as an error. Zero delivered reviews after a sign-in response must not be presented as "this product has no reviews".

Use the observed page sample for the requested marketplace, and report access failures separately. This Actor does not sign in or promise access to the full archive.

What a variable public-page sample is useful for

An accessible sample can support these uses, provided its size and marketplace limitations stay visible:

  • Complaint detection across accessible products. Costs follow delivered rows: 1,000 review rows cost $5.00. Optional product summaries add $0.003 each. Do not estimate volume by assuming a fixed number of reviews per ASIN.
  • Rating trends per product. The optional $0.003 product row carries rating, ratings_count and reviews_visible when shown. It can be charged even when an accessible product has no review rows. Changes in ratings count are not a count of new written reviews.
  • Competitor monitoring. Track the sample currently visible on accessible product pages. Keep review IDs for deduplication and do not treat the sample as a complete or representative archive.
  • Verified-purchase filtering. Every row carries the verified flag, the helpful votes, the variant reviewed and the country the review was written in ("Reviewed in ..." — that is the country of writing, not the reviewer's residence).

What it is not good for: longitudinal review-text analysis, review-fraud studies that need the full distribution, or anything that needs reviews older than the page shows.

Working code

from apify_client import ApifyClient

client = ApifyClient("<YOUR_APIFY_TOKEN>")
run = client.actor("kestrel/amazon-reviews-scraper").call(run_input={
    "asins": ["https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B09G9FPHY6"],
    "maxReviewsPerProduct": 3, "requireText": True, "includeProductRow": True,
})
for row in client.dataset(run["defaultDatasetId"]).iterate_items():
    if row["type"] == "product":
        print(row["title"], row["rating"], "from", row["ratings_count"], "ratings;",
              row["reviews_visible"], "reviews on the page")
    elif row["type"] == "review":
        print(row["rating"], row["country"], row["verified"], row["text"][:80])

For a catalogue, keep the marketplace with each ASIN. This example uses the UK marketplace; US review requests are currently sign-in blocked. Search results do not guarantee that reviews will be public:

search = client.actor("kestrel/amazon-search-scraper").call(run_input={
    "queries": ["mechanical keyboard"], "pages": 2, "domain": "co.uk"})
asins = [r["asin"] for r in client.dataset(search["defaultDatasetId"]).iterate_items()
         if r["type"] == "product" and r.get("asin")]
client.actor("kestrel/amazon-reviews-scraper").call(run_input={
    "asins": asins[:100], "domain": "co.uk", "minRating": 3,
    "maxReviewsPerProduct": 3, "includeProductRow": False})

Review titles or text can be absent. Use requireText: true to exclude rows without written text before billing.

Blocks versus ceilings, and how the run tells them apart

A captcha or sign-in page is an access failure, not an empty product. The Actor reports failed targets without charging review rows. The verified UK examples demonstrate current access to those pages only; they do not establish that all marketplaces or all UK products will work.

Check the status and error before interpreting zero delivered reviews. Retrying does not guarantee that a sign-in gate will disappear.

Prices are a separate question

Amazon withholds price markup from unauthenticated clients often enough that you should not build a price tracker on the product page. On one 1.8 MB page in testing there was no price element at all. When a price is rendered it is the buybox offer only — not the marketplace's cheapest seller, not used offers, and null on pages where no buying option is shown.

Prices and ranks are reliably rendered on result and chart pages, which is where Amazon Search and Amazon Best Sellers read them. If you want price tracking, use those; if you want product attributes, images and stock, use Amazon Product.

FAQ

How many Amazon reviews can a scraper actually get per product?

There is no guaranteed count. Two UK product pages tested on 2026-11-07 exposed 5 and 8 reviews before filters and caps; these are observations, not bounds. Current US requests require sign-in. A sign-in response is an error, not evidence that the product has no reviews.

Why can ratings_count exceed the number of returned reviews?

ratings_count is the overall rating count reported by Amazon; reviews_visible is the observed sample on the page. Filters and caps may reduce delivered reviews further. Both fields are in the optional product row at $0.003 when includeProductRow is true; review rows cost $0.005 each.

Can I get more reviews with a residential proxy or a browser?

Neither guarantees additional reviews or full history. Public-page samples and sign-in gates are separate limitations. UK examples currently work, while tested US reviews require sign-in; proxy changes do not establish access to the archive.

Why is price often null on Amazon review runs?

Amazon frequently withholds price markup from unauthenticated clients — on one 1.8 MB product page in testing there was no price element at all. Treat price on a review run as a bonus. Prices and ranks are rendered reliably on search and chart pages, which is where the search and best-sellers scrapers read them.
